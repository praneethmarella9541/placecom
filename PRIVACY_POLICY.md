## 1. Information We Collect

We collect information you provide, information obtained from connected services, and technical information generated when you use the Service.

### 1.1 Account and profile information

Depending on how your account is provisioned and which features you use, we may collect and store:

- Name, display name, username, email address, and Supabase account identifier.
- Authentication provider information, including Google account/provider metadata where you sign in with Google.
- Password credentials handled by Supabase Auth. We do not receive or store your plaintext password.
- Role, team or group membership, mailbox ownership, feature restrictions, job title, biography, and account timestamps.
- Mobile phone number, assigned business or virtual telephone number, and communication preferences.
- Mobile push-notification token and device/platform information when push notifications are enabled.
- Account activity and security information needed to authenticate users, maintain sessions, enforce permissions, and investigate misuse.

### 1.2 Information from Google and other connected accounts

If you connect Google services, we may access and process the data and permissions you authorize, including:

- Gmail messages, threads, labels, drafts, attachments, sender and recipient information, subjects, message bodies, and related metadata.
- Google Calendar events, attendees, invitations, availability/free-busy information, meeting links, and responses.
- Google Drive files, folders, metadata, permissions, uploads, downloads, and shared-link information.
- Google Contacts and People API information, including names, email addresses, phone numbers, organizations, and profile photos where available.
- Google Forms definitions, questions, responses, and related metadata.
- Google Sheets workbooks, sheets, cell data, and related metadata.
- OAuth access and refresh tokens required to maintain authorized connections. These tokens are stored server-side and are not intended to be stored in browser local storage.

The exact information available depends on the Google scopes you approve and the features enabled for your account. You can revoke Google access through your Google account settings; revocation may disable connected features until you reconnect the account.

### 1.3 CRM, recruiting, and operational information

The Service may collect information entered by users or imported from connected systems, including:

- Candidate, recruiter, employer, customer, partner, and other contact names.
- Personal and business email addresses, phone numbers, company names, job titles, notes, tags, scores, stages, and interaction histories.
- Email-derived contact records and extracted names, email addresses, phone numbers, and grouping information.
- Meeting schedules, attendees, meeting URLs, notes, recordings, transcripts, transcript segments, summaries, and related identifiers.
- Forms, responses, documents, spreadsheets, attachments, and files uploaded or created through the Service.

### 1.4 Calling, SMS, WhatsApp, and messaging information

When these features are enabled, we may collect and process:

- Telephone numbers, sender and recipient addresses, business-line assignments, call direction, call status, duration, notes, and provider identifiers.
- Call recordings, recording URLs, transcriptions, transcript segments, and related technical metadata.
- SMS and WhatsApp message bodies, delivery status, timestamps, conversation identifiers, read status, template names, stars, pins, media URLs, media filenames, and uploaded media content.
- Incoming webhook data from communications providers, including delivery reports and provider event identifiers.

You must have an appropriate legal basis and any required consent before using the Service to record calls, send communications, import contacts, or process information about other people. You are responsible for honoring applicable calling, messaging, recording, marketing, and anti-spam laws.

### 1.5 Usage, device, and log information

We and our service providers may collect:

- Pages and routes viewed, page-leave events, feature usage, and interactions with the Service.
- Browser, device, operating-system, platform, approximate network information, and diagnostic information.
- IP address, timestamps, request metadata, authentication events, error reports, webhook events, and server logs.
- Usage quantities and internal cost estimates for certain automated services, including OpenAI token usage and communications usage.

### 1.6 Cookies and browser storage

The Service uses cookies and similar technologies for authentication, session continuity, security, and application functionality. Supabase-managed session cookies may be created, refreshed, or cleared by the browser session and middleware.

The application also uses browser local or session storage for functional preferences and short-lived caches, including some combination of:

- Drive view mode and sorting preferences.
- Inbox layout preferences and cached mail lists or threads.
- Sidebar state.
- Extraction-job progress and related temporary state.
- Mailbox/profile caches.
- Notification, extraction, email-limit, label, and WhatsApp-template preferences.

These storage mechanisms are primarily functional rather than advertising cookies. Disabling cookies or browser storage may prevent sign-in or impair parts of the Service. The application currently does not intentionally store Google or Supabase API credentials in ordinary local storage or session storage, although session behavior is partly managed by the Supabase client library.

## 4. Data Storage, Security, and Retention

### 4.1 Storage

Information may be stored in Supabase PostgreSQL tables, Supabase Storage buckets, Google services, communications-provider systems, analytics systems, hosted application infrastructure, backups, and device/browser storage. Data location and international transfer details depend on the production configuration of each provider.

The application includes row-level security policies and role-based checks for many user-owned records, and server-only handling for privileged service-role credentials. Authentication sessions are refreshed through server middleware. However, no software system is completely secure, and access-control policies and provider configurations must be reviewed and maintained by the operator.

### 4.2 Security practices

We use reasonable technical and organizational safeguards appropriate to the nature of the information, which may include:

- Encrypted HTTPS transport for web and API requests where configured.
- Supabase authentication and session cookies.
- Server-side storage of service credentials and OAuth refresh tokens.
- Supabase row-level security and application role checks.
- Restricted server routes for privileged operations.
- Access logging, error monitoring, and administrative controls.

You are responsible for protecting your account credentials, reviewing connected-account permissions, using the Service only from trusted devices, and promptly reporting suspected unauthorized access. We cannot guarantee absolute security.

### 4.3 Retention

While an account or organization workspace is active, we retain information for as long as reasonably necessary to provide the Service, maintain business and security records, meet legal obligations, resolve disputes, enforce agreements, and protect the Service.

Following closure of an account or an organization's workspace, we retain associated data in our production systems for **90 days**, after which it is deleted or de-identified. This period allows for accidental-closure recovery, outstanding legal or business matters, and orderly export before deletion.

The 90-day period does not guarantee immediate deletion from routine backups or third-party providers that independently control data, including Google, OpenAI, Exotel, and Vercel. Backup retention is governed by the applicable backup cycle. Some feature-specific deletion controls are available before account closure, and WhatsApp deletion may initially be soft deletion. The current codebase does not yet demonstrate automated enforcement of the 90-day deletion window; the operator must implement and audit that process.

If you use the Service on behalf of an organization, that organization may be the controller or business responsible for information processed in its workspace. In that case, direct privacy requests may need to be submitted to the organization first, and The Nucleus may process the information on its instructions.
